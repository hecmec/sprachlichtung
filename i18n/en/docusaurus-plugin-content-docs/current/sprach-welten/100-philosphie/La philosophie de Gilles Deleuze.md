## **A structured summary: the philosophy of Gilles Deleuze, a creative revolution**

<!--t src=0e113567-->
### **Introduction: Deleuze, an earthquake in philosophy**
To study Gilles Deleuze is to venture into the heart of an **explosive** body of thought that unsettles the traditional foundations of philosophy. His last book, *What Is Philosophy?* (1991, co-written with Félix Guattari), proposes a radical redefinition of the discipline. For Deleuze, philosophy is neither a science nor an art, but an **activity of pure creation**: it invents concepts, as art creates works and science creates functions. This idea, revolutionary though it is, belongs to a line of thinkers (Spinoza, Nietzsche) who saw philosophy as a force for **intensifying life** rather than for explaining it.

<!--t src=369f9c34-->
## **1. Philosophy between science and art: a third way**
Traditionally, human thought is divided into two poles:
- **Science**: it explains the world through laws and rational demonstrations.
- **Art**: it expresses emotions and visions, without seeking to demonstrate anything.

<!--t src=a4ef3fd8-->
**The problem**: where does philosophy stand? Historically it has always been on the side of science, favouring logic and argument. But Deleuze **turns this perspective on its head**: philosophy is not a quest for truth, but a **creation of concepts**. The philosopher is no longer a scholar, but an **artist of thought**.

<!--t src=118a852d-->
**Examples**:
- Plato interrogated concepts such as "the soul" or "love".
- Kant, Descartes and Nietzsche each forged concepts with which to think the world.
- **But**: nobody had asked *what a concept itself is*.

<!--t src=e7713365-->
For Deleuze, a concept is not a simple idea or a word. It is an **original construction**, an invention that **transforms the way we see and live**. To philosophise is not to search for truth, it is **to create tools for intensifying existence**.

<!--t src=843756ba-->
## **2. Philosophy as an affirmation of life**
Deleuze draws on **Spinoza** (the power to act) and **Nietzsche** (the destruction of old concepts) to argue that philosophy must **let life pass into thought**. This means:
- Refusing to freeze reality into definitions.
- Plunging into the **flux of life** in order to espouse its movement.
- **Taking part in the creation of the world**, rather than merely commenting on it.

<!--t src=a8b566c0-->
**The consequence**: thought becomes an **active force**, not a mere mirror of reality.

<!--t src=773d865c-->
## **3. Against identity: schizophrenia as a metaphor**
Deleuze criticises our obsession with identity. We spend our lives constructing a stable "self" (e.g. "I am a teacher, married, father of two"), but this identity is a **prison**.

<!--t src=f8dbdaa9-->
**A provocative thesis**:
- Schizophrenia is not an illness to be avoided but an **original state**: we are all assemblages of "blocks of desire" that society forces us to organise into a coherent "self".
- **Example**: falling in love at first sight is not an encounter between two identities, but the formation of an **assemblage** (a flow of desire running through the individuals).
- **The problem**: society (family, state, work) pushes us to **code** these flows of desire in order to turn them into a fixed identity.

<!--t src=410f2e7e-->
**The solution**: philosophy must help us to **decode** these flows, to reorganise them freely, without sinking into madness. It becomes an **art of living**, a way of **becoming** rather than of **being**.

<!--t src=10f4cefa-->
## **4. Desire: a factory, not a lack**
For Deleuze, desire is not a lack (as in psychoanalysis) but a **productive force**:
- It does not seek to possess, but to **connect** (e.g. the baby's mouth plugging into the mother's breast).
- We are **desiring machines**, run through by flows that plug in and unplug continuously.
- **The problem**: the "self" acts as a **customs officer**, limiting the connections available to us.

<!--t src=cc452b91-->
**What is at stake**: learning to **let desire circulate** without blocking it in a frozen identity.

<!--t src=587c9086-->
## **5. Thinking like a rhizome, not like a tree**
Deleuze contrasts two models of thought:
- **The tree**: traditional, linear thought, centred on origins and identity (e.g. "I am this way because I had that childhood").
- **The rhizome**: a network without a centre, in which any point can connect to any other (e.g. ginger, which grows in all directions).

<!--t src=af3488de-->
**Application**:
- **Live your life as a geography, not as a history.**
  - A **history** locks us into a narrative ("I am like this because of my past").
  - A **geography** opens us to **possible trajectories** ("Where am I now? What assemblages can I create?").
- **Example**: after a break-up, instead of looking for a cause ("It's my fault"), accept the **deterritorialisation** (the event that tears us away from our habits).

<!--t src=106a46f8-->
**The goal**: to trace **lines of flight**, escape routes out of the "self", in order to invent new ways of living.

<!--t src=2bb5d8cc-->
## **6. Chaosmos: order and chaos intertwined**
Deleuze invents the concept of **chaosmos** (chaos + cosmos):
- The **cosmos** is not the opposite of chaos, but a **temporary assemblage** extracted from disorder.
- Philosophical concepts are **islands of meaning** wrested from chaos, but they do not abolish it.

<!--t src=a77b5531-->
**Key quotation** (Nietzsche, taken up by Deleuze):
*"One must still have chaos in oneself to be able to give birth to a dancing star."*

<!--t src=dc1a17f2-->
**Implication**:
- Philosophy should not seek to **tame** chaos, but to **espouse** it in order to draw creation from it.
- **Example**: an artist creates a work out of chaos; a philosopher invents a concept that **stands up** amid instability.

<!--t src=faee086b-->
## **7. Freedom: becoming rather than choosing**
For Deleuze, freedom is not a conscious choice ("What do I want to become?"), but a **capacity to let life run through you**.
- **To be free** is:
  - Not to resist the events that destabilise us.
  - To **become** with others, with ideas, with situations, without trying to control everything.
  - To turn your body into a **place of passage** for the flows of desire.

<!--t src=868ceadf-->
**A concrete example**:
- Falling in love at first sight is not an encounter between two identities, but the birth of a **new circuit** between two beings.
- Philosophy teaches us to **surf on these flows**, rather than trying to freeze them.

<!--t src=b5ca90ca-->
## **Conclusion: philosophy as an art of living**
Deleuze redefines philosophy as:
1. A **creation of concepts** (not an explanation).
2. An **art of living** (not a theory).
3. An **affirmation of chaos** (not a flight into order).

<!--t src=33753bd5-->
**Legacy**:
- He invites us to **go beyond our identities** in order to explore unprecedented **becomings**.
- Thought is no longer a tool for understanding the world, but a **force for reinventing it**.

<!--t src=e4fa2f2e-->
**A final question**:
What if philosophising were, above all, **learning to dance with chaos**?

<!--t src=c6b77ce2-->
**To go further**:

- *Mille Plateaux* (A Thousand Plateaus, for more on the rhizome).
- *Nietzsche et la philosophie* (Nietzsche and Philosophy, to understand Nietzsche's influence).

**Questions**:

- Contrast the existentialist idea of freedom with Deleuze's. (conscious choice vs. the capacity to let oneself be run through)
