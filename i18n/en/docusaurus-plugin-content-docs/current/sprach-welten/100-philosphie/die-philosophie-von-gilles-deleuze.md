## **A structured summary: The philosophy of Gilles Deleuze, a creative revolution**

<!--t src=045fc4a5-->
### **Introduction: Deleuze, an earthquake in philosophy**

<!--t src=074daae0-->
To study Gilles Deleuze is to venture into the heart of an **explosive** body of thought, one that shakes the inherited foundations of philosophy. His last work, _What Is Philosophy?_ (1991, written together with Félix Guattari), proposes a radical redefinition of the discipline. For Deleuze, philosophy is neither a science nor an art, but an **activity of pure creation**: it invents concepts, just as art brings forth works and science brings forth functions. Subversive as this thought is, it nevertheless stands in a line of thinkers (Spinoza, Nietzsche) who saw in philosophy a force for **intensifying** life rather than explaining it.

<!--t src=15ff0f19-->
## **1. Philosophy between science and art: a third way**

<!--t src=b5c6806c-->
Conventionally, human thought divides into two poles:

<!--t src=a69a2dc9-->
- **Science**: it explains the world through laws and rational demonstration.
- **Art**: it expresses feelings and ways of seeing, without seeking to prove anything.

<!--t src=eebeb8e5-->
**Problem**: Where does philosophy stand? Historically it always sided with science, giving precedence to logic and argument. Deleuze, however, **inverts this perspective**: philosophy is not a search for truth but a **creation of concepts**. The philosopher is no longer a scholar, but an **artist of thought**.

<!--t src=35950995-->
**Example**:

<!--t src=211ebe5e-->
- Plato interrogated concepts such as "the soul" or "love".
- Kant, Descartes and Nietzsche each coined concepts for thinking the world.
- **But**: nobody had asked _what a concept itself actually is_.

<!--t src=de9d99d4-->
For Deleuze a concept is no mere idea and no mere word. It is an **original construction**, an invention that **transforms our way of seeing and living**. To philosophise is not to search for truth, but to **create tools that intensify existence**.

<!--t src=280af5a2-->
## **2. Philosophy as an affirmation of life**

<!--t src=57ac18e0-->
Deleuze draws on **Spinoza** (the power to act) and **Nietzsche** (the destruction of old concepts) to claim that philosophy must **let life pass over into thought**. That means:

<!--t src=6ba8d0eb-->
- Refusing to let reality freeze into definitions.
- Plunging into the **stream of life** in order to follow its movement.
- **Taking part in the creation of the world**, instead of merely commenting on it.

<!--t src=77c7539c-->
**Consequence**: Thought becomes an **active force**, not a mere mirror of reality.

<!--t src=7072d1c7-->
## **3. Against identity: schizophrenia as a metaphor**

<!--t src=23a461fa-->
Deleuze criticises our obsession with identity. We spend our lives building a stable "I" (e.g.: "I am a teacher, married, a father of two"), yet this identity is a **prison**.

<!--t src=bae7861b-->
**A provocative thesis**:

<!--t src=22765a05-->
- Schizophrenia is not an illness to be avoided but an **original condition**: we are all assemblages of "blocks of desire" that society forces us to order into a coherent "I".
- **Example**: Love at first sight is not an encounter between two identities, but the formation of an **assemblage** (a stream of desire running through individuals).
- **Problem**: Society (family, state, work) presses us to **code** these streams of desire in order to turn them into a fixed identity.

<!--t src=e4b79ba1-->
**Solution**: Philosophy should help us to **decode** these streams and reorder them freely, without falling into madness. It becomes an **art of living**, a mode of **becoming** rather than of **being**.

<!--t src=dd4f5186-->
## **4. Desire: a factory, not a lack**

<!--t src=f9651d53-->
For Deleuze desire is not a lack (as in psychoanalysis) but a **productive force**:

<!--t src=6665936b-->
- It does not seek to possess but to **connect** (e.g.: the infant's mouth attaching to the mother's breast).
- We are **desiring machines**, run through by streams that continually couple and uncouple.
- **Problem**: The "I" acts like a **customs officer** and limits the connections available to us.

<!--t src=db9350d8-->
**What is at stake**: learning to **let desire circulate**, without blocking it in a rigid identity.

<!--t src=50a0d65f-->
## **5. Thinking like a rhizome, not like a tree**

<!--t src=1da717ab-->
Deleuze sets two models of thought against each other:

<!--t src=03807342-->
- **The tree**: inherited, linear thought revolving around origin and identity (e.g.: "I am this way because I had this childhood").
- **The rhizome**: a network without a centre, in which every point can connect with every other (e.g.: ginger, which grows in all directions).

<!--t src=7df767a3-->
**Application**:

<!--t src=3f8af8ec-->
- **Live your life as a geography**, not as a history.
  - A **history** locks us into a narrative ("I am this way because of my past").
  - A **geography** opens us to **possible paths** ("Where am I now? Which assemblages can I create?").
- **Example**: After a separation, do not search for a cause ("It is my fault"), but accept the **deterritorialisation** (the event that tears us out of our habits).

<!--t src=350717b6-->
**Aim**: to draw **lines of flight**, escapes from the "I", in order to invent new ways of living.

<!--t src=0418ccad-->
## **6. The chaosmos: order and chaos interlaced**

<!--t src=5860b55b-->
Deleuze invents the concept of the **chaosmos** (chaos + cosmos):

<!--t src=c3b87bba-->
- The **cosmos** is not the opposite of chaos, but a **provisional assemblage** wrested from disorder.
- Philosophical concepts are **islands of meaning**, torn from chaos, yet they do not abolish it.

<!--t src=4c2b6d70-->
**Key quotation** (Nietzsche, taken up by Deleuze):
_"One must still have chaos in oneself to be able to give birth to a dancing star."_

<!--t src=d4925452-->
**Implication**:

<!--t src=3093044d-->
- Philosophy should not **tame** chaos but **embrace** it, in order to win creation from it.
- **Example**: An artist makes a work out of chaos; a philosopher invents a concept that **holds up** amid instability.

<!--t src=cf7ec67f-->
## **7. Freedom: becoming instead of choosing**

<!--t src=14e60334-->
For Deleuze freedom is not a conscious choice ("What do I want to become?"), but a **capacity to let oneself be traversed by life**.

<!--t src=1fc5361f-->
- **To be free** means:
  - Not resisting the events that throw us off balance.
  - To **become** with others, with ideas, with situations, without wanting to control everything.
  - To turn one's own body into a **place of passage** for the streams of desire.

<!--t src=958f748c-->
**A concrete example**:

<!--t src=f601b5ed-->
- Love at first sight is not an encounter between two identities, but the birth of a **new circuit** between two beings.
- Philosophy teaches us to **surf** these streams, without wanting to fix them in place.

<!--t src=cdb64e77-->
## **Conclusion: Philosophy as an art of living**

<!--t src=953094dc-->
Deleuze redefines philosophy as:

<!--t src=4d055bdc-->
1. A **creation of concepts** (not an explanation).
2. An **art of living** (not a theory).
3. An **affirmation of chaos** (not a flight into order).

<!--t src=057a2c40-->
**Legacy**:

<!--t src=28a55eab-->
- He invites us to **go beyond our identities** in order to explore unheard-of **modes of becoming**.
- Thought is no longer a tool for understanding the world, but a **force for reinventing it**.

<!--t src=196c2f1e-->
**A closing question**:
What if philosophising meant above all **learning to dance with chaos**?
