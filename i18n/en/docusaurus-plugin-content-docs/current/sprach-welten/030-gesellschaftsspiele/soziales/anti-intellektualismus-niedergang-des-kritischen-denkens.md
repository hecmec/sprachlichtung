## 1. Definitions and framing

<!--t src=6195e7e1-->
- **Anti-intellectualism**: a social attitude that systematically undermines science-based facts, academic and institutional authority, and the pursuit of theory and knowledge. She names three strands:
  - religious anti-intellectualism (emotion/faith over reason),
  - populist anti-elitism (rejection of elite institutions and intellectuals),
  - instrumentalism (knowledge only counts if it's practical).
- **Historical stakes**: anti-intellectualism has long been a tool of **totalitarian power** (she cites Hitler, Franco, Pinochet, Maduro, Stalin, Milošević). Opens with the Asimov quote about the American "cult of ignorance" and the false notion that "my ignorance is just as good as your knowledge."
- **Critical thinking** is framed as the companion skill: analyzing, weighing multiple views, not jumping to conclusions.

<!--t src=172fdcdc-->
**Central question:** why are critical thinking, media literacy, and reading comprehension at such a low point? She proposes two main causes.

<!--t src=b2ea766e-->
## 2. Cause 1: The long devaluation of the arts and humanities

<!--t src=68e829f8-->
- **Cultural messaging**: "most useless majors" listicles, parental discouragement, the "arts degree = McDonald's job" joke. Students (herself included at 17) choose majors on financial return, not interest.
- **Historical contrast**: humanities were once central — classical education in Greece and Rome aimed at forming informed, virtuous citizens; science and philosophy were intertwined. Examples: Einstein crediting Hume's _Treatise_ as inspiration for relativity; Borges's fiction anticipating set theory, recursion, chaos theory; Hippocrates shaping medicine.
- **Capitalism as driver**: from Adam Smith's division of labor onward, hyper-specialization split science from philosophy; by the 21st century STEM and humanities were oceans apart. Value became whatever the market pays. Humanities degrees fell ~25% between 2012 and 2020.
- **Universities complicit**: market-driven education prioritizes profitable, industry-aligned programs (CS, engineering, business) while defunding humanities — a self-reinforcing cycle. She notes student debt makes the ROI-first choice rational, so she doesn't blame individuals.
- **Why it matters**: humanities train close reading, writing, and argumentation — asking _why_ something is said, defending and justifying ideas, discovering that there's more than one correct way of thinking. Their devaluation explains readers who can't connect fantasy to real-world issues or who insist "books shouldn't be political."
- **Clarification**: she's not claiming non-humanities majors can't think critically — the problem is that the _activities_ (slow deep reading, defending an idea) are now dismissed as impractical.

<!--t src=bf6c3a6d-->
## 3. Cause 2: Modern digital media

<!--t src=7cf93660-->
- **Attention economy**: skimming, scrolling, constant multitasking; exposure to many ideas but deep engagement with none. Attention is monetized by "those who master distraction" (quoting John Westenberg).
- **Reading habits**: platform-style skimming erodes linear reading; boredom intolerance leads to complaints about long paragraphs, DNF-ing, chasing "the good stuff."
- **Clickbait incentives**: clicks/likes/shares as currency push outlets and creators toward sensational titles and thumbnails over quality; commenters react to titles without watching.
- **The "never-ending now"**: most consumed content was made in the last 24 hours; novelty beats timelessness (Plato and Tolstoy are a click away, yet ignored). The 24-hour cycle rewards speed over accuracy and pressures creators to publish under-researched takes — producing confident surface-level knowledge.
- **Misinformation asymmetry** (Brandolini's law, cited via "Raline is low"): refuting nonsense takes an order of magnitude more energy than producing it, so people accept rather than verify. Boomers/Facebook and Gen Z/TikTok are equally credulous.
- **Confirmation bias + algorithms**: platforms optimize for gratification; disagreeable or difficult content gets scrolled past and stops being recommended.
- **Outsourced opinion**: people read comment sections to form views before thinking for themselves — even though commenters are an unrepresentative slice of viewers (silent watchers don't register).
- **Echo chambers**: any bias can find a validating community (e.g., the incel-radicalization pathway); bubbles create artificial confidence and the shock of the US election results for those who never engaged with the other side.

## 4. Symptoms in online discourse (esp. book communities)

<!--t src=9456cde0-->
- **Winning over debating**: people want to _be right_ and prove others stupid rather than exchange arguments; questioning one's beliefs feels shameful, so debate is avoided.
- **Deflection phrases**: "just let people enjoy things," "it's not that deep," "sometimes the curtains are just blue" — used as shields against any criticism. Her counter: enjoying something and critiquing it aren't mutually exclusive, and deep analysis is _her_ idea of fun.
- **Offense-seeking and hyper-individualism**: treating any critical view of a work as a personal attack on one's escapism, which shuts down conversation.
- **Case study — _Onyx Storm_ / Rebecca Yarros fandom**: superfans attacking anyone who voices reasonable criticism, with a montage of bad-faith defenses ("my brain was off, that's why I loved it," "it got me back into reading," "criticism = elitism/censorship/negativity," "the author is a good person").
- **Case study — the Céline video**: the TikTok that popularized the topic became an example of the problem itself — death threats and personal vilification instead of disagreement, until the creator stopped discussing it. The pattern generalizes to fandoms, celebrities, and politics: mild controversy → pile-on → creators self-censor → conversations die.

## 5. Conclusion

<!--t src=2cce92c3-->
No neat resolution — she finds the situation frustrating and doesn't know if it will change. Closing points:

<!--t src=cd87f3a9-->
- Being critical of media and enjoying it are compatible (she is proof).
- Be wary of anyone claiming absolute truth who leaves no room for questioning — because **intellectuals always question**.
