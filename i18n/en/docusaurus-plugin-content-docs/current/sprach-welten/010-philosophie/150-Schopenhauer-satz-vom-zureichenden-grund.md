**Schopenhauer: _Über die vierfache Wurzel des Satzes vom zureichenden Grunde_ (On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason)**

<!--t src=16cac1da-->
## Summary

<!--t src=f6458c62-->
Schopenhauer's early dissertation (1813, revised 1847) examines the "principle of sufficient reason": the insight that nothing is without a ground. He criticises the unclear use of this principle by his predecessors (especially Wolff) and systematically arranges it into four distinct roots, according to the mode of cognition involved.

<!--t src=787763c5-->
### 1. The General Thesis

<!--t src=4f342ebc-->
The principle of sufficient reason states: _Nothing is without a ground why it is._ Yet "ground" means different things depending on the kind of object presented to the subject. Schopenhauer distinguishes four kinds of ground that cannot be reduced to one another.

<!--t src=f5ed8b62-->
### 2. The Four Roots

<!--t src=e1074cb2-->
1. **Ground of becoming (causality in experience)**
   - Concerns changes in the world of the senses.
   - Every change has a cause.
   - The foundation of natural science: the linking of appearances in space and time.

<!--t src=91b9e3b9-->
2. **Ground of knowing (logical justification)**
   - Concerns judgements and inferences.
   - A judgement is true only if it follows from other, already accepted judgements.
   - Here the principle appears as the logical principle of justification.

<!--t src=5c2f0a71-->
3. **Ground of being (space and time)**
   - Space and time are a priori forms of order in which every element receives its position through the others.
   - A point in space can be determined only through its relation to other points; likewise a moment through its relation to other moments.

<!--t src=14167dbb-->
4. **Ground of acting (motivation)**
   - Concerns the conduct of the subject as a being of will.
   - Every action arises from a motive that necessarily leads to the act of will.
   - This forms the foundation of ethics and psychology.

<!--t src=9587428a-->
### 3. The Significance of the Distinction

<!--t src=7348b161-->
- The fourfold meanings prevent confusion between logical, physical, mathematical and practical justification.
- The principle is not uniform, but formally identical while differing in content.
- Schopenhauer thereby combines Kantian epistemology (space and time as forms of intuition, causality as a form of the understanding) with a systematic analysis of the concept of grounding.

<!--t src=d6f434a5-->
### 4. Its Further Function

<!--t src=1b886b34-->
- The text provides the epistemological foundation for Schopenhauer's main work (_Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung_ / The World as Will and Representation).
- Above all the fourth root, motivation, opens the way to his later metaphysics of the will.

<!--t src=58b63e27-->
---

<!--t src=08d4f635-->
**Core idea:**
Schopenhauer breaks the principle of sufficient reason down into four irreducible types of grounding (becoming, knowing, being, acting), thus giving a precise, systematic ordering of the conditions under which something "has a ground".

:::info Quote
μάλιστα μὲν καὶ πρῶτον δόξειε τὸ μηδὲν ἀναιτίως γίγνεσθαι, ἀλλὰ κατὰ προηγουμένας αἰτίας.

Plutarch, _De fato_ ("On Fate"), conclusion (ch. 11), as a doctrine of the Stoics.

<!--t src=edff6483-->

"Above all and first of all it would seem to hold that nothing comes to be without a cause, but rather in accordance with antecedent causes."

<!--t src=a61508ba-->
:::info Quote
πᾶσα γὰρ ἐπιστήμη διανοητικὴ ἢ μετέχουσά τι διανοίας περὶ αἰτίας καὶ ἀρχάς ἐστιν.

Aristotle, _Metaphysics_ VI (E) 1, 1025b 6–7.
:::

<!--t src=608930c5-->
Translated literally:
"For every science that involves thought, or that has some share in thought, is concerned with causes and principles."

<!--t src=226e5119-->
More freely in English:
"All science that rests on thinking, or that partakes of thinking at all, deals with causes and first principles."

<!--t src=2f8e2a83-->
:::info Quote
ἐπίστασθαι δὲ οἰόμεθα ἕκαστον ἁπλῶς, ὅταν τήν τ’ αἰτίαν οἰώμεθα γινώσκειν, δι’ ἣν τὸ πρᾶγμά ἐστιν, ὅτι ἐκείνου αἰτία ἐστίν, καὶ μὴ ἐνδέχεσθαι τοῦτο ἄλλως εἶναι.

Aristotle, _Analytica Posteriora_ (Posterior Analytics) I, 2, 71b 9–12.
:::

<!--t src=aba950a2-->
Translation:
"We think we know a thing in the strict sense only when we believe we know the cause through which the thing is, and know that it is the cause of that thing, and that it cannot be otherwise."

<!--t src=58b63e27-->
---

<!--t src=49d62a1f-->
:::info Quote
αἰτίαι δὲ τέσσαρες· μία μὲν τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι· μία δὲ τὸ τίνων ὄντων ἀνάγκη τοῦτο εἶναι· ἑτέρα δὲ ἡ τί πρῶτον ἐκίνησε· τετάρτη δὲ τὸ τίνος ἕνεκα.

Aristotle, _Analytica posteriora_ (Posterior Analytics) II, 11 (94a 20 ff.).
:::

<!--t src=225a06f4-->
Translation into English:
"There are four causes:

<!--t src=1cda3e1a-->
1. the essence, or the what-it-was-to-be (τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι = the essence / the form),

<!--t src=59659c14-->
2. that out of which something is, insofar as it is necessary that it be out of it (τὸ τίνων ὄντων ἀνάγκη τοῦτο εἶναι = the matter),

<!--t src=5e76cacf-->
3. that which first set something in motion (ἡ τί πρῶτον ἐκίνησε = the moving, or efficient, cause),

<!--t src=9abb166b-->
4. that for the sake of which something is (τὸ τίνος ἕνεκα = the end, or purpose)."

<!--t src=2b798c60-->
👉 In the customary terminology they are therefore called:

<!--t src=34d1e1d0-->
**Formal cause** (causa formalis)

<!--t src=ce96d72d-->
**Material cause** (causa materialis)

<!--t src=73dde8d9-->
**Efficient cause** (causa efficiens)

<!--t src=6d2024fb-->
**Final cause** (causa finalis)
